# Subsurface Ocean Temperature Reconstruction (SIH) 

## - <mark>The Problem:</mark> 

* Satellites take great photos of the ocean surface (temperature, height, saltiness, wind). 

* But satellites cannot see underwater (below 0 meters). 

* Direct underwater sensors (ARGO buoys) are sparse—like having 5 thermometers across the entire Bay of Bengal. 

## - <mark>The Analogy:</mark> 

* Looking at a boiling pot from above. Steam and bubbles on top give clues about how hot the bottom is. 

* In the ocean, a slight bulge in surface height (SLA) + warm surface (SST) usually means a thick pocket of warm water underneath. 

## - The Goal: 

* Build an AI model that takes 2D satellite surface maps and predicts a 3D block of ocean temperature across 15 depth levels (0m to 1000m) for the North Indian Ocean. 

## 2. TECHNICAL SETUP (HOW IT ACTUALLY WORKS) 

- Inputs (7 Surface Channels): 

1. Sea Surface Temperature (SST) 

2. Sea Surface Salinity (SSS) 

3. Sea Level Anomaly / Height (SLA/SSH) 

4. Surface Currents U (East-West) 

5. Surface Currents V (North-South) 

6. Surface Winds U 

7. Surface Winds V 

## - Outputs (15 Depth Channels): 

* Temperature grids at depths: 0m, 5m, 10m, 20m, 30m, 50m, 75m, 100m, 125m, 150m, 200m, 300m, 500m, 700m, 1000m. 

## - Model Architecture: 

* Think of it as Image-to-Image translation (U-Net ) //ConvNeXt 

- Input tensor:  [Batch_Size, 7 channels, Height, Width] 

- Output tensor: [Batch_Size, 15 channels, Height, Width] 

## 3. ADVANTAGES & DISADVANTAGES OF THIS APPROACH 

## ADVANTAGES: 

- Continuous 3D Visibility: Fills in the massive spatial and temporal gaps left by physical buoys. 

- Real-Time Capability: Once trained, the AI generates daily subsurface maps in seconds from fresh satellite feeds. 

- High Impact: Directly helps cyclone intensity prediction (cyclones feed on subsurface heat content) and marine heatwave tracking. 

## DISADVANTAGES: 

- Physics Blind Spots: Standard AI models can hallucinate impossible physics (e.g., predicting 30°C water at 1000m depth where it should be near freezing). 

- Extreme Event Error: AI struggles during rare, unseen events like super-cyclones where the surface-subsurface relationship temporarily decouples. 

- Resolution Limits: Small-scale ocean eddies smaller than the 0.25° grid (~25 km) will be smoothed out. 

## 4. REAL CHALLENGES 

## 1. NetCDF (.nc) Data Hell: 

* Satellite data comes in massive `.nc` files with different coordinate systems, grids, and timestamps. 

* Solution: Use Python's `xarray` and `rioxarray` to re-grid everything to a standard 0.25° x 0.25° grid before training. 

## 2. Irregular Land Boundaries: 

* The North Indian Ocean is bounded by complex coastlines (India, Arabian Peninsula, Southeast Asia). 

* Problem: The AI will try to predict ocean temperatures on dry land. 

* Solution: Apply a strict binary land-sea mask (set land values to 0 or NaN and exclude them from loss calculation). 

## 3. Hardware & Memory Bottlenecks: 

* Multi-year daily ocean grids consume tens of gigabytes of RAM. 

* Solution: Do not load full history at once. Train on a 3 to 5-year window (e.g., 2018–2022) using PyTorch custom Dataset with on-the-fly chunk loading. 

4. Vertical Inversion Violations: 

* In normal oceanography, warm water sits on top and cold water sits at the bottom. A standard neural network might predict colder water above warmer water in deep layers. 

* Solution: Add a penalty term (Physics-Informed Loss) if T(depth_k) < T(depth_k+1).

## 2.2. 3D Ocean temperature 

The ocean temperature data used as the ground truth in this study were obtained from the Global Ocean Ensemble Physics Reanalysis of the Copernicus Marine Data Store (European Union-Copernicus Marine Service Citation2019). <mark>This product was generated by a numerical ocean model constrained by data assimilaton from satellites and in situ observatons, providing a physically consistent and gap-free 3D ocean state estmate. This product provides daily averages at a 0.25° resoluton and covers the period from 1993 to the present. It encompasses 75 vertcal levels, ranging in depth from approximately 0.5 m to nearly 6000 m, as shown in Table 1.</mark> This product exhibits high accuracy, with a global mean bias of less than 0.1 °C and a root mean square difference of approximately 0.49 °C averaged over the full 0–5000 m (Bourdallé-Badie and Carabin, Citation2024). 

## <mark>1. What Already Exists</mark> 

- **Traditional Numerical Physics Models (Operational):** 

   - Systems like **GLORYS / NEMO** (Copernicus Marine) and **INCOIS ROMS/GODAS** (India) simulate full 3D ocean physics using supercomputers. 

   - _The catch:_ These take hours to days of compute time, require complex data assimilation, and cannot run real-time "nowcasts" in a matter of seconds. 

- **Statistical / Classical Grids:** 

   - **ARMOR3D** and **ISAS** : Statistical tools that project surface anomalies down into the water column using historical correlations and Argo float mapping. 

   - _The catch:_ They use linear/statistical projections that struggle with non-linear monsoon dynamics and eddy turbulence in the Arabian Sea / Bay of Bengal. 

- **Academic Deep Learning Research (Papers & Datasets):** 

   - Recent research papers (like **DORS** , ConvLSTM models, and FSF-Net) have demonstrated that CNNs and Vision Transformers can map surface satellite fields to subsurface profiles. 

- _The catch:_ These are research proofs-of-concept (often monthly averages or 1° coarse resolutions), not streamlined, daily operational software pipelines. 

## **<mark>What This Project Demands</mark>** 

- **Instant inference** (< 1 second on a standard GPU) 

- **High-resolution daily feeds** (0.25° grid) 

- **Specialized North Indian Ocean tuning** (capturing freshwater influx in the Bay of Bengal & salinity shifts) 

- **End-to-end usable product** (ingestion _→_ inference _→_ interactive UI) 

There are nearly **4,000 active Argo floats** drifting in the world's oceans. While individual units cost about $20,000, the system is remarkably **cost-effective** overall. 

## **<mark>Problems and Limitatons of Argo</mark>** 

- **Limited Lifespan:** Batteries last roughly 3 to 5 years before the floats stop working. 

- **Coverage Gaps:** Shallow marginal seas, heavy sea-ice zones, and remote polar regions are harder to sample. 

- **Drift Control:** Floats drift with ocean currents and cannot be easily steered to specific target locations. 

- **Sensor Drift:** Sensors can degrade or lose calibration over years underwater. 

- **End-of-Life Waste:** Dead floats eventually sink to the seafloor, releasing minor amounts of battery and material waste.

## **Step-by-Step Production Roadmap** 

## **1.Data Acquisition and Pipeline Harmonization:** Prerequisite for all model training. 

1. Download daily surface files (SST, SSS, SLA, Winds, Currents) and target GLORYS reanalysis temperature files for a fixed multi-year window (e.g., 2019–2023). 

2. Crop all variables strictly to the North Indian Ocean bounding box (5 _∘_ N to 30 _∘_ N _,_ 45 _∘_ E to 105 _∘_ E). 3. Bilinearly interpolate all datasets to a uniform 0.25 _∘ ×_ 0.25 _∘_ spatial grid. 

4. Generate a binary land mask (water = 1, land = 0) and normalize all channels to zero-mean and unit-variance. 

5. Save processed data into compressed HDF5/Zarr format for fast disk-to-GPU training. 

## **2.Model Architecture and Training:** Core AI development. 

1. Construct a 2D **U-Net** or **ConvNeXt-based Encoder-Decoder** where: 

   - **Input:** Tensor of shape [Batch_Size, 7, Height, Width] (7 surface variables). 

   - **Latent Space:** An embedding layer that captures spatial eddy and thermocline dynamics. 

   - **Output:** Tensor of shape [Batch_Size, 15, Height, Width] representing the 15 standard depth layers. 

   2. Train using a masked **Mean Squared Error (MSE)** loss function that completely ignores land coordinates. 

   3. Incorporate a vertical physics-informed regularizer to penalize inverted temperature profiles where deep water is predicted hotter than surface layers. 

- **3.Independent ARGO Validation Suite:** Proves scientific credibility to judges. 

   1. Ingest raw in-situ vertical profile data from INCOIS / ARGO floats within the region. 

   2. Extract the model's predicted vertical temperature profile at the exact float coordinates and timestamps. 

   3. Compute and plot: 

      - Vertical RMSE curves from 0 m down to 1000 m. 

      - Pearson correlation coefficients across the water column. 

      - Ocean Heat Content (OHC) error estimation for cyclone tracking.
