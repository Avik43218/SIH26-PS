# AI-Powered Mobile Urban Intelligence Platform Using Public Transport Fleet

## 1. Problem Statement Overview

**Problem Statement ID:** 26124
**Title:** AI-Powered Mobile Urban Intelligence Platform Using Public Transport Fleet

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. Executive Summary

Cities already have a large network of public transport buses travelling
through almost every major road every day. Modern buses are increasingly
equipped with multiple cameras covering the front, rear, sides and
passenger cabin. At present, these cameras are mostly treated as passive
recording devices and are primarily used for post-incident
investigation.

The core problem is that cities lack a continuously moving, city-wide
sensing layer capable of observing road conditions, traffic,
infrastructure and public-safety situations in real time.

Traditional approaches depend heavily on:

-   Fixed CCTV cameras
-   Manual road inspections
-   Traffic police observations
-   Citizen complaints
-   Periodic infrastructure surveys

These approaches are expensive, limited in geographic coverage and often
slow to respond. A pothole, damaged road divider, missing zebra crossing
or waterlogged road may remain unnoticed until someone reports it.
Similarly, traffic congestion may develop between fixed camera
locations, leaving authorities with incomplete situational awareness.

The proposed solution is to transform buses into **mobile AI-powered
urban sensing units**. Cameras already installed on buses can
continuously capture the environment while the bus follows its normal
route. AI models running directly on the bus can analyse video streams,
identify relevant events and send only important metadata or alerts to a
centralized urban intelligence platform.

This creates a distributed sensing network without requiring a
completely new city-wide camera infrastructure.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. What Has to Be Built

The solution should consist of two major parts:

## A. Edge-AI Onboard System

A software stack running inside each bus that:

1.  Receives video from multiple cameras.
2.  Processes video in real time.
3.  Detects road defects and hazards.
4.  Detects, classifies and counts vehicles.
5.  Estimates traffic density.
6.  Identifies traffic bottlenecks.
7.  Detects vulnerable pedestrian situations.
8.  Detects suspicious incidents such as hit-and-run or rash driving.
9.  Tracks vehicles involved in incidents.
10. Performs Automatic Number Plate Recognition (ANPR/ALPR).
11. Assigns confidence scores to detections.
12. Adds timestamp and GPS coordinates.
13. Filters duplicate or low-confidence events.
14. Compresses/selects relevant evidence.
15. Sends alerts and metadata to the central platform.

## B. Centralized Urban Intelligence Platform

A cloud/server-based platform that:

1.  Receives data from the bus fleet.
2.  Stores events and historical observations.
3.  Displays events on a GIS map.
4.  Generates road-condition maps.
5.  Generates congestion heat maps.
6.  Shows incident locations.
7.  Provides fleet-wide traffic analytics.
8.  Identifies infrastructure deficiencies.
9.  Analyses route delays.
10. Estimates origin-destination traffic patterns.
11. Generates reports.
12. Provides actionable information to transport and municipal
    authorities.

# 4. Main Objectives

The system should achieve the following objectives:

### 4.1 Turn Buses into Mobile Sensors

Instead of installing thousands of additional fixed cameras, use
existing bus cameras as moving sensors.

### 4.2 Detect Road Problems Automatically

Identify:

-   Potholes
-   Cracks and damaged road surfaces
-   Missing road dividers
-   Damaged road dividers
-   Missing zebra crossings
-   Damaged zebra crossings
-   Missing traffic signs
-   Damaged traffic signs
-   Waterlogging
-   Debris
-   Other road hazards

### 4.3 Understand Traffic

The system should detect:

-   Cars
-   Buses
-   Trucks
-   Motorcycles
-   Auto-rickshaws
-   Bicycles
-   Other relevant vehicle classes

It should then calculate:

-   Vehicle count
-   Vehicle density
-   Traffic flow
-   Approximate speed
-   Queue length
-   Congestion level
-   Bottleneck locations

### 4.4 Improve Public Safety

Detect situations involving:

-   Pedestrians crossing roads
-   Children near roads
-   School-zone activity
-   Pedestrians at risk of collision
-   Dangerous vehicle behaviour
-   Rash driving
-   Potential hit-and-run incidents

### 4.5 Support Incident Investigation

For relevant incidents, the system should record:

-   Incident type
-   Timestamp
-   GPS coordinates
-   Vehicle track
-   Vehicle class
-   Number plate
-   Number-plate confidence
-   Detection confidence
-   Short evidence clip or selected frames
-   Camera ID
-   Bus ID
-   Route information

### 4.6 Provide City-Wide Intelligence

The centralized platform should combine observations from all buses to
understand the city as a continuously changing system.

# 7. Edge Video Processing

Sending every frame from every camera to the cloud would consume large
amounts of bandwidth.

Therefore, the majority of AI processing should happen on the bus.

### Edge processing pipeline

``` text
Camera
  ↓
Frame Capture
  ↓
Pre-processing
  ↓
Object Detection
  ↓
Object Tracking
  ↓
Specialized AI Models
  ↓
Event Classification
  ↓
Confidence Filtering
  ↓
GPS + Timestamp
  ↓
Event Packaging
  ↓
Local Storage
  ↓
Secure Transmission
```

The bus should transmit primarily:

-   Event metadata
-   Detection results
-   Small image snippets
-   Relevant evidence frames
-   Short video clips for high-priority incidents

Instead of continuously uploading raw video.

Detection alone is not sufficient because the system must understand


This is important for:

-   Vehicle counting

# 10. Traffic Detection and Density Estimation

The system should count vehicles passing through defined regions or
virtual lines.

Example:

``` text
         Road
────────────────────────
       ↓ Car
       ↓ Bus
       ↓ Truck

──────── Virtual Line ────────

Vehicle Count = 37
Average Speed = 18 km/h
Density = High
Congestion = Severe
```

The platform can calculate:

``` text
Traffic Density =
Number of detected vehicles /
Estimated road segment area
```

More sophisticated traffic-flow algorithms can also use:

-   Vehicle trajectories
-   Speed
-   Lane occupancy
-   Queue length
-   Vehicle arrival rate

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 11. Traffic Bottleneck Detection

A bottleneck can be detected when traffic characteristics remain
abnormal over a period of time.

For example:

``` text
Normal:
Speed = 40 km/h
Vehicles = 25/min

Congested:
Speed = 12 km/h
Vehicles = 80/min
Queue = 200 m
```

A congestion score can be created using:

``` text
Congestion Score =
weighted combination of

- Vehicle density
- Average speed
- Queue length
- Lane occupancy
- Historical traffic baseline
```

The system should compare current traffic with historical patterns to
distinguish normal peak-hour traffic from unusual congestion.

# 12. Pedestrian and Vulnerable Road User Detection

The system should detect pedestrians and identify potentially dangerous
situations.

Possible scenarios:

-   Children crossing roads
-   Large groups crossing roads
-   Pedestrians crossing outside designated crossings
-   Pedestrian close to moving vehicles
-   School-zone activity
-   Pedestrian-vehicle conflict

A simple risk model could consider:

``` text
Risk =
Distance between pedestrian and vehicle
+
Relative speed
+
Direction of movement
+
Time-to-collision
```

High-risk events should generate alerts.

# 13. Rash Driving Detection

Rash driving should not be determined solely from one frame.

The system should analyse vehicle motion over time.

Possible indicators:

-   Sudden acceleration
-   Sudden braking
-   Abrupt lane changes
-   Dangerous overtaking
-   High relative speed
-   Repeated weaving
-   Unsafe proximity to other vehicles

The system can combine:

``` text
Object Detection
       +
Tracking
       +
Trajectory Analysis
       +
Speed Estimation
       +
Behaviour Classification
```

# 14. Hit-and-Run Detection

This is one of the more complex modules.

``` text

       ↓

       ↓

       ↓

       ↓

       ↓

       ↓
```

# 15. ANPR / Number Plate Recognition

ANPR should use a two-stage process.

### Stage 1: Number Plate Detection

Locate the registration plate.

### Stage 2: OCR

Read characters from the detected plate.

Potential tools:

-   PaddleOCR
-   EasyOCR
-   Tesseract
-   Custom OCR model

The output should look conceptually like:

``` json
{
  "plate": "WB01AB1234",
  "confidence": 0.91,
  "vehicle_type": "car",
  "timestamp": "2026-08-23T13:04:15",
  "gps": {
    "latitude": 22.5726,
    "longitude": 88.3639
  }
}
```

The actual plate format and validation rules should be adapted to the
deployment region.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 16. GPS and Location Intelligence

Every important event should be geographically tagged.

Required fields:

-   Latitude
-   Longitude
-   Timestamp
-   Bus ID
-   Route ID
-   Camera ID
-   Direction
-   Optional road-segment ID

This allows events to be visualized on a GIS map.

Example:

``` text
Pothole
Location: Road Segment A
GPS: 22.xxxxxx, 88.xxxxxx
Severity: High
Detected by: Bus 102
Time: 08:42 AM
```

Repeated detections of the same defect should be merged into one
persistent road issue.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 17. Duplicate Event Handling

A major challenge is that multiple buses may detect the same pothole.

For example:

``` text
Bus 101 → Pothole A
Bus 103 → Pothole A
Bus 109 → Pothole A
Bus 117 → Pothole A
```

The backend should cluster these observations geographically.

Possible matching criteria:

-   GPS distance
-   Road segment
-   Object category
-   Image similarity
-   Time window

Result:

``` text
Persistent Road Defect
Confidence: 96%
Observed: 14 times
First observed: Monday
Last observed: Friday
```

This greatly improves reliability.

### High

-   Dangerous traffic behaviour

### Medium

-   Damaged sign

# 52. Key Challenges

## Challenge 1 --- Weather

Rain, fog and low light reduce detection accuracy.

### Solution

Train using diverse environmental conditions and use image enhancement
where appropriate.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Challenge 2 --- Motion Blur

Moving buses create blur.

### Solution

Use appropriate shutter speed, robust models and temporal tracking.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Challenge 3 --- Network Loss

Buses may lose connectivity.

### Solution

Local queue + persistent storage + retry mechanism.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Challenge 4 --- False Positives

AI may mistake shadows or road markings for defects.

### Solution

Use:

-   Confidence thresholds
-   Temporal consistency
-   Multiple observations
-   Geographic clustering
-   Human verification for critical decisions

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Challenge 5 --- Edge Compute Limitations

Multiple cameras can overload the device.

### Solution

Use:

-   Model optimization
-   TensorRT
-   Frame sampling
-   ROI processing
-   Hardware acceleration

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Challenge 6 --- Duplicate Detections

Multiple buses detect the same problem.

### Solution

Use PostGIS spatial clustering and observation aggregation.

# 57. Conclusion

The proposed system solves a major urban-management problem by
converting an existing public transport fleet into a distributed network
of intelligent mobile sensors.

Instead of relying only on fixed CCTV infrastructure and manual
inspections, buses continuously observe roads while performing their
normal transportation duties. Edge AI converts raw video into structured
information such as road defects, traffic density, pedestrian risks and
incidents. A centralized platform then combines these observations
across the entire fleet and turns them into GIS maps, heat maps,
analytics and actionable alerts.

The most important architectural principle is to perform AI processing
at the edge and transmit only useful information to the central
platform. This reduces bandwidth and latency while allowing the system
to scale across a large fleet.

A successful implementation should therefore combine:

**Computer Vision + Edge AI + Multi-Object Tracking + ANPR + GPS + IoT
Communication + GIS + Spatial Databases + Cloud/Server Analytics +
Secure APIs + Real-Time Dashboards.**

The ultimate goal is to move from:

> **Reactive city monitoring**

to:

> **Proactive, AI-driven urban intelligence.**
